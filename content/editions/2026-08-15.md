## SCULPT: Subtractive Composition for 3D Part Generation

- Authors: Sikuang Li, Chen Yang, Jiemin Fang, Jiazhong Cen, Yuhe Wei, Jichen Pang, Wei Shen, Qi Tian
- Affiliations: Not provided
- Tier: Read first
- Lane: Graphics / 3D / 4D
- Review depth: Full paper
- Estimated reading time: 8 minutes

### Abstract

SCULPT begins with a complete object in TRELLIS.2's structured 3D latent space and repeatedly predicts one extracted part together with the updated remainder. Coupled denoising, overlapping native sparse supports, and an explicit empty-remainder state make part count adaptive while retaining geometry, materials, and a shared object frame.

### Verdict

The standout graphics paper today: subtractive part–remainder generation is an elegant way to obtain editable, variable-cardinality 3D assets without sacrificing the coherence of the assembled whole.

### Contribution

SCULPT initializes from a complete TRELLIS.2 latent, then reuses one coupled flow-transformer interface to denoise an extracted part and its remainder at every step. Native sparse supports may overlap at interfaces, while composition loss, support clipping, and an explicit empty state keep the rollout grounded in the original object.

### Why it matters

Part-aware asset creation is more useful than monolithic image-to-3D output for animation, materials, fabrication, and editing. Modeling the remainder explicitly shifts boundary completion into the generator and turns unknown part count into rollout length rather than a fixed slot budget.

### Evidence

On 200 PartObjaverse objects, SCULPT reports part-level Chamfer distance 0.0107 and F1@.05 0.7599 versus 0.0136 and 0.7025 for OmniPart. Reassembled-object Chamfer improves from 0.0032 to 0.0020, and F1@.05 from 0.8732 to 0.9212; it also narrowly improves object geometry over TRELLIS.2 plus PartField.

### Limitation

The quantitative benchmark is small and close to the curated Objaverse-derived training domain; out-of-domain evidence is only six examples. Training uses 32 GPUs for 800,000 stage-wise steps, and an unresolved remainder at the safety cap becomes an extra part rather than demonstrating learned semantic termination.

## GeoCache: Training-Free Acceleration of Multi-View Texture Diffusion via Geometric Delta Transport

- Authors: Haotang Li, Zhenyu Qi, Shaohan Henry Wang, Kebin Peng, Yutong Zhao, Zi Wang, Bo Liu, Huanrui Yang, Sen He
- Affiliations: Not provided
- Tier: Read first
- Lane: Graphics / 3D / 4D
- Review depth: Full paper
- Estimated reading time: 8 minutes

### Abstract

GeoCache accelerates multi-view texture diffusion without training by denoising a subset of anchor views and transporting their per-step clean-signal changes through geometry correspondences. Target views retain their own state, while periodic full-view passes restore regions poorly covered by the anchors.

### Verdict

A sharp, graphics-specific acceleration idea: reuse across geometrically corresponding views instead of blindly caching along diffusion time, where missed multi-view harmonization creates seams.

### Contribution

GeoCache computes each denoising step for anchor views, transports the anchor's change in predicted clean signal through rendered position-map correspondences, and adds that delta to each target view's retained state. Periodic all-view evaluation re-grounds poorly covered surfaces, and sampler-specific conversion preserves the expected solver parameterization.

### Why it matters

Multi-view texture diffusion repeatedly processes the same surface from different cameras, but conventional temporal caches discard the cross-view corrections that keep texture coherent. Geometry-aware delta transport exploits the actual redundancy while remaining training-free and portable across backbones.

### Evidence

On Hunyuan3D-2.1 Paint, the main setting reports a 2.21-times denoiser-loop speedup with MV-LPIPS 0.0293 and MV-PSNR 33.60, outperforming other methods above two-times speedup on fidelity. On MVPainter it reports 3.61-times loop speedup with MV-LPIPS 0.0240; results also cover SyncMVD and a distilled substrate.

### Limitation

The impressive figures primarily describe denoiser-loop savings: the default Hunyuan pipeline improves only 1.11 times for painting and 1.07 times end to end. Accuracy depends on position-map correspondence and anchor visibility, and performance degrades on long trajectories or sparsely observed surfaces.

## EgoPHI: Estimating Contact and Force from Egocentric Vision

- Authors: Andela Ilic, Rachel Schuchert, Yijing Jiang, Christian Holz
- Affiliations: Not provided
- Tier: Read first
- Lane: Graphics / 3D / 4D
- Review depth: Full paper
- Estimated reading time: 8 minutes

### Abstract

EgoPHI predicts dense contact maps and normal-force distributions on hand and object meshes from one egocentric RGB frame plus known object geometry. Physics simulation supplies scalable per-vertex supervision, while graph reasoning and iterative pose refinement align the interacting meshes before force estimation.

### Verdict

A compelling vision–graphics intersection: it moves monocular hand–object understanding from contact labels toward dense physical interaction on both meshes, and backs simulation supervision with a small real sensing study.

### Contribution

EgoPHI combines image features, hand and object geometry, graph-based interaction reasoning, and iterative pose refinement to predict per-vertex contact and normal force. A SOFA-based pipeline augments existing interaction data, while FTIR-instrumented objects provide real force maps for validation.

### Why it matters

Dense forces expose how an object is manipulated, not merely where it is touched, supporting imitation, grasp analysis, and physically grounded interaction reconstruction. The combination of simulation, geometric registration, and real sensing is a useful pattern for supervision that is otherwise nearly impossible to capture at scale.

### Evidence

On ARCTIC, EgoPHI reduces hand-force MAE from HACO's 6.62 N to 4.03 N and predicts object forces unavailable to the baseline. Its simulated-versus-real validation reports per-frame MAE of 0.36 N for a cylinder and 0.25 N for a cube, with Spearman correlations 0.760 and 0.604 respectively.

### Limitation

The model is single-frame, requires an object mesh, assumes uniform stiffness, predicts only normal force, and lacks mass or other properties needed for global equilibrium. Real evaluation covers just a cube and cylinder from eight participants, so transfer to curved, compliant, or heterogeneous objects is unproven.

## PixSDS: Why Latent SDS Makes Noisy Pixels

- Authors: Vsevolod Skorokhodov
- Affiliations: Not provided
- Tier: Read first
- Lane: Graphics / 3D / 4D
- Review depth: Abstract
- Estimated reading time: 7 minutes

### Abstract

PixSDS diagnoses structured texture artifacts in latent score distillation as VAE-induced pixel drift: images can move along weakly encoded pixel-space directions while their latents remain clean. Its lightweight repair decodes a latent lookahead and uses that image as a VAE-consistent optimization direction.

### Verdict

A valuable diagnosis-and-repair paper for text-to-3D: it explains why latent-space SDS can look semantically correct to the model while visibly accumulating high-frequency pixel artifacts.

### Contribution

PixSDS identifies weakly constrained directions in the VAE encoder as the source of pixel drift, then decodes a latent SDS lookahead and uses that clean image as a gradient-repair direction in pixel optimization without retraining the prior or changing the renderer.

### Why it matters

Many 3D generation pipelines inherit SDS artifacts without a clear causal account. A lightweight, VAE-consistent correction can be immediately relevant to optimization-based graphics pipelines and may generalize beyond this particular objective.

### Evidence

The abstract cites controlled 2D SDS tests, VAE-only optimization, simplified analysis, and text-to-3D experiments that substantially reduce structured artifacts while preserving semantics; public code is linked. It does not provide numerical margins in the abstract.

### Limitation

This brief reviewed the abstract rather than the full experiments, so the size, consistency, and compute cost of the gains remain unchecked. The method also retains the base SDS objective and therefore does not address its other geometric or semantic failure modes.

## LocusGS: Spatially Grounded Tokens for Feed-Forward 3D Gaussian Splatting

- Authors: Wenyu Li, Sidun Liu, Tongrui Hu, Peng Qiao, Yong Dou
- Affiliations: Not provided
- Tier: Read first
- Lane: Graphics / 3D / 4D
- Review depth: Abstract
- Estimated reading time: 7 minutes

### Abstract

LocusGS gives each query token an explicit 3D center and support radius that are refined through the decoder. Anchor-to-ray bias gathers locally relevant multi-view observations, and anchor-centered decoding keeps each token's Gaussians spatially coherent rather than scattering them across the scene.

### Verdict

A clean geometric correction to feed-forward 3DGS: explicit spatial state gives query tokens the locality that a purely latent representation does not reliably learn.

### Contribution

LocusGS augments every Gaussian query with a refined 3D center and radius, uses anchor-to-ray bias to gather spatially relevant observations, and decodes Gaussians around the anchor so query groups occupy coherent local regions.

### Why it matters

Query-based reconstruction is easier to inspect and control when each token corresponds to a genuine scene region. Spatial grounding may improve not only rendering but also part selection, editing, and structured downstream reasoning over Gaussian scenes.

### Evidence

The abstract reports improved novel-view rendering over query-based Gaussian-token baselines under the same Gaussian budget, together with analyses showing coherent anchor layouts and more structured Gaussian groups. The project page provides an interactive viewer for anchor groups and comparisons.

### Limitation

The abstract gives no numerical gains, dataset breakdown, runtime, or comparison with stronger non-query feed-forward methods. Whether the explicit anchors help downstream editing or semantics is plausible but not evaluated in the material reviewed here.

## Structure-aware Riemannian Growth Fields for 4D Plant Modeling

- Authors: Meng-Yu Jennifer Kuo, Ryo Kawahara
- Affiliations: Not provided
- Tier: Worth skimming
- Lane: Graphics / 3D / 4D
- Review depth: Abstract
- Estimated reading time: 4 minutes

### Abstract

This work models plant development as continuous geodesic flow constrained by botanical structure, combining symbolic growth rules with Riemannian geometry. A new ten-day, dual-species dataset provides dense geometry and semantic annotations for evaluating shape evolution and temporal correspondence.

### Editorial note

This paper treats 4D plant reconstruction as structured growth rather than independent per-time geometry, combining symbolic botanical rules with geodesic flow to preserve hierarchy and correspondence. A new ten-day, dual-species dataset with dense geometry and semantics is useful in its own right, and the WACV submission reports better geometry and correspondence than prior methods. Exact gains, sensitivity to sparse observations, and generalization beyond two species need full-paper inspection.

## Towards Physics-Faithful Generation of Scientific Diagrams

- Authors: Minghui Zhang, Jinxin Shi, Yifan Chang, Liangliang Zhao, Yuandong Pu, Qian Yu, Ming Hu, Hanxiao Zhang, Yun Gu, Yirong Chen, Yu Qiao, Bo Zhang, Xiangchao Yan, Bin Fu, Yihao Liu
- Affiliations: Not provided
- Tier: Worth skimming
- Lane: Broader vision
- Review depth: Abstract
- Estimated reading time: 4 minutes

### Abstract

Princigram trains scientific-diagram generation with a fixed, subdiscipline-specific reasoning schema that separates visible facts from physical inferences and types mathematics symbolically. The accompanying pipeline annotates millions of physics images and evaluates each result through item-specific questions about forces, objects, and states.

### Editorial note

Princigram targets an important failure hidden by visual plausibility: generated physics diagrams can have wrong forces, coordinates, states, or equations. Its structured physical reasoning schema supports 4.3 million curated images, 115,037 expert-level annotations, and a fact-decomposed benchmark derived from each diagram's own annotation. The abstract says structured supervision improves faithfulness but omits numerical margins, and model-judge dependence makes human verification important.

## PlayWorld: Benchmarking World Models with Agent Players over Long-Horizon Objectives

- Authors: Kaixin Ding, Xi Chen, Minghong Cai, Zhiyuan Xu, Yiyang Wang, Yuxiang Lu, Junyi Li, Shuyang Chen, Yuan Gao, Xin Tao, Pengfei Wan, Hengshuang Zhao
- Affiliations: Not provided
- Tier: Worth skimming
- Lane: Graphics / 3D / 4D
- Review depth: Abstract
- Estimated reading time: 4 minutes

### Abstract

PlayWorld evaluates interactive video world models by asking multimodal agent players to pursue 171 long-horizon objectives rather than replaying one fixed action sequence. It measures geometry consistency, interaction fidelity, and whether visible and hidden scene state evolves persistently.

### Editorial note

PlayWorld replaces fixed action replay with multimodal agents that adapt their actions to achieve the same long-horizon goal in different world models. Its 171 scenarios separate geometry consistency, interaction fidelity, and persistent evolution of visible and hidden state; nine current models remain unreliable, especially spatially and over time. The agent itself becomes part of the measurement loop, so robustness to player policy and evaluator bias is the main methodological question.

## AutoDesign: Meta-Harness Optimization for Long-Horizon Agentic Design

- Authors: Yaxin Luo, Haobin Jiang, Jialv Zou, Xu Huang, Wenhao Yan, Haodong Li, Zhengrong Yue, Jing Li, Xiaofu Chen, Xiaohan Zhao, Jiacheng Liu, Jiacheng Cui, Zhiqiang Shen, Xiaotong Li
- Affiliations: Not provided
- Tier: Worth skimming
- Lane: General AI
- Review depth: Abstract
- Estimated reading time: 4 minutes

### Abstract

AutoDesign uses a meta-harness optimizer to revise the tools and instructions around a code agent from rollout feedback. On academic paper-to-poster generation, the learned harness improves performance across several agent-model configurations and supports long autonomous editing trajectories.

### Editorial note

AutoDesign treats the surrounding agent harness as an object that can improve recursively from rollout feedback, demonstrated on paper-to-poster generation. Across seven agent-model configurations, the learned harness raises the average PosterBench score from 54.99 to 67.39; one 40-minute run uses 253 tool calls and 11 edits for under $3. Because the benchmark and optimizer come from the same work and the task is narrow, independent replication is needed before generalizing the result to agentic design broadly.
