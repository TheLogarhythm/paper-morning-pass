## A Geodesic Cut-Cell Prior for Neural Skinning

- Authors: Wenchao Ma, Surya Dwarakanath, Yizhak Ben-Shabat, Dario Kneubühler, Haomiao Jiang, Sharon X. Huang, Hsueh-Ti Derek Liu
- Affiliations: Not provided
- Tier: Read first
- Lane: Graphics / 3D / 4D
- Review depth: Full paper
- Estimated reading time: 8 minutes

### Abstract

Cut-cell skinning supplies neural skinning models with a fast graph approximation of volumetric geodesic distance. Axis-aligned ray intersections and winding-number queries build a graph that preserves separation between nearby surface parts, enabling a scalable geometric prior for in-the-wild meshes.

### Verdict

The strongest graphics paper today: a simple, production-minded geometric prior materially improves several neural skinning systems while remaining fast enough for large training corpora.

### Contribution

The method approximates volumetric geodesics with a cut-cell graph built from axis-aligned ray casts and winding-number classification, then converts bone-to-vertex graph distances into normalized skinning weights that can replace or augment priors in RigNet, UniRig, and Puppeteer.

### Why it matters

Purely learned skinning captures semantics but can generalize poorly, while classical geometric weights are robust but expensive or semantically blind. This work demonstrates a practical hybrid in which geometry supplies locality and separation and the network handles material and shape semantics.

### Evidence

On the de-duplicated Articulation-XL 2.0 split, adding the prior to UniRig reduces average L1 error from 0.768 to 0.447 and deformation error from 25.29 to 14.18. Cut-cell graph construction takes 0.029 seconds at resolution 64 in the reported CPU benchmark, versus 2.50 seconds for Mandoline and 13.37 seconds for fTetWild on the tested subset.

### Limitation

Winding-number inside/outside classification still assumes a roughly closed volume and can fail on inverted triangles or thin shells. Disconnected components fall back to Euclidean nearest-joint assignment, which can bind them to unrelated nearby joints, and the finer graph costs more geodesic computation than cage representations.

## Seed2GS: Camera-Free, Training-Free Object Extraction from 3D Gaussian Scenes via a Single Reference-View Grounding

- Authors: Zongjian Ding, Yudong Gao, Jiale Liu, Xinglin Yu, Junxing Ren, Dong Wei, Yajing Chen, Shan Huang, Mingjun Cheng, Min Li
- Affiliations: Not provided
- Tier: Read first
- Lane: Graphics / 3D / 4D
- Review depth: Full paper
- Estimated reading time: 8 minutes

### Abstract

Seed2GS extracts an object from a frozen Gaussian-splat scene without the original reconstruction cameras or scene-specific representation training. It fixes identity in one grounded reference mask, lifts a conservative seed into 3D, expands coverage through visibility-adaptive virtual orbits and tracking, and fits one temporary foreground logit per Gaussian.

### Verdict

A highly practical 3DGS editing primitive: it separates the one-time question of object identity from multi-view coverage, avoiding both per-scene feature training and dependence on missing reconstruction cameras.

### Contribution

Seed2GS selects one reference mask from several open-vocabulary proposals, conservatively lifts it to Gaussians, renders visibility-adaptive virtual orbits, propagates the seed through tracking, and optimizes only a temporary foreground logit while freezing the scene representation.

### Why it matters

Prebuilt Gaussian assets often omit original images and cameras, yet editing still requires a clean object subset. A query-time extraction tool that leaves the representation untouched makes transform, removal, duplication, and recoloring workflows considerably more accessible.

### Evidence

The paper reports 92.1% mIoU on LERF-MASK at 9.3 seconds mean query latency, 3.7 points above the strongest scene-trained baseline and 7.6 points above the closest camera-free baseline, plus 95.7% mIoU on 3D-OVS. Its fixed-reference control retains 91.1% mIoU, and the trajectory and propagation ablations account for substantial gains.

### Limitation

A bad initial grounding propagates through the whole pipeline; severe occlusion, same-class ambiguity, absent geometry, and large viewpoint shifts remain failure modes. The favorable-reference sweep does not represent arbitrary views, and runtime excludes process startup and scene/model loading.

## LiveAnimate: Stable Long-Form Streaming Human Animation in Real-Time

- Authors: Yuxuan Zhang, Haozhong Xiong, Yubo Huang, Jiayi Song, Jinpeng Yu, Haofan Wang, Jiaming Liu, Ruihua Huang, Liwei Wang
- Affiliations: Not provided
- Tier: Read first
- Lane: Graphics / 3D / 4D
- Review depth: Full paper
- Estimated reading time: 8 minutes

### Abstract

LiveAnimate converts a 14-billion-parameter bidirectional video diffusion transformer into a block-causal three-step generator for pose-driven streaming animation. A bounded cache combines a permanent reference anchor, pose-retrieved history, and a short rolling window to resist long-horizon identity and appearance drift.

### Verdict

A strong systems-and-model contribution for interactive animation: it makes bounded-memory, long-horizon diffusion streaming concrete rather than treating speed and stability as separate demonstrations.

### Contribution

LiveAnimate first adapts a bidirectional DiT into a reference-anchored block-causal model, then distills it under self-generated history to three denoising steps. Its PR-Sink cache retains the first block, one pose-matched historical block, and three recent blocks with position-consistent key reuse.

### Why it matters

Interactive avatars require constant latency and identity stability over minutes, not just attractive short clips. The combination of training against rollout errors, selective long-range retrieval, and explicit parallel-system optimization provides a useful recipe beyond this particular avatar model.

### Evidence

On a 24-pair, three-minute benchmark, the paper reports 19.63 FPS for 12-frame blocks on two H100 GPUs, with 0.611-second steady-state block latency and bounded memory. Final-segment DINO similarity falls from 0.818 to 0.805 without the dynamic sink and to 0.693 without the static sink, supporting their complementary roles.

### Limitation

Generation is currently limited to 480-by-480, one person, and little camera motion. The real-time figure relies on two H100 GPUs and excludes independently pipelined VAE decoding from the critical-path timing, so ordinary-hardware deployment remains distant.

## StateFlow: Building, Evolving, and Accessing 3D World States for Previsualization

- Authors: Yuyang Yin, Zixiang Li, Longxuan Deng, Hongkai Li, Shifang Zhao, Junnan Liu, Weirong Huang, Mengyu Wang, Tianxiao Fu, Yikai Wang, Peng-Shuai Wang, Xiaojie Jin, Yao Zhao, Yunchao Wei
- Affiliations: Not provided
- Tier: Read first
- Lane: Graphics / 3D / 4D
- Review depth: Abstract
- Estimated reading time: 7 minutes

### Abstract

StateFlow organizes generative previsualization around a persistent, editable 3D world state rather than one-shot video synthesis. It constructs a world from generated views, applies structured state transitions while retaining memory, and uses render feedback to refine camera trajectories before optional video-model enhancement.

### Verdict

A timely generative-graphics direction that makes an editable 3D state—not a prompt or a video—the persistent source of truth for iterative previsualization.

### Contribution

StateFlow constructs a coherent world from generated dual views, maps user edits into structured state transitions that preserve prior scene memory, and uses rendered feedback to correct camera plans before higher-fidelity video synthesis.

### Why it matters

Film, game, and design previsualization is inherently iterative and spatial. An explicit world state offers edit locality, reusable cameras, and inspectable geometry that one-shot video generators cannot provide reliably.

### Evidence

The abstract reports high-quality 3D worlds for video creation and game-like prototyping and links a project page, but provides no numerical comparison in the abstract.

### Limitation

The available abstract does not quantify 3D consistency, edit success, or user-study outcomes, so the apparent controllability advantage needs full-paper and project-demo inspection.

## Surfsvr: 2D Surface Priors as 3D Geometric Regularizers for Sparse Voxel Reconstruction

- Authors: Yan Di, Chengxi Li, Yaoxing Wang, Mengge Liu, Zhigang Li, Ruida Zhang, Mingyang Li, Pengyuan Wang, Shan Gao, Xiangyang Ji
- Affiliations: Not provided
- Tier: Worth skimming
- Lane: Graphics / 3D / 4D
- Review depth: Abstract
- Estimated reading time: 4 minutes

### Abstract

SurfSVR converts coherent 2D surface regions into persistent 3D regularizers for sparse voxel reconstruction. Planar or quadratic surface models guide voxel subdivision, depth and normal optimization, pruning, and floater suppression in weakly textured or sparsely observed areas.

### Editorial note

SurfSVR turns image-space surface coherence into persistent constraints throughout sparse-voxel reconstruction rather than lifting noisy depth pointwise. Its planar-or-quadratic region models guide subdivision, optimization, pruning, and floater removal, and the abstract reports consistent state-of-the-art results on three public benchmarks. The absence of quantitative margins and unreleased code make this a method to inspect, not yet a result to take at face value.

## Map-Det3D: Metric Feed-Forward 3D Reconstruction Prior for Multi-view 3D Object Detection from Streaming Inputs

- Authors: Yung-Hsu Yang, Luigi Piccinelli, Samuel Rota Bulò, Sunghwan Hong, Denis Rozumny, Johannes Schönberger, Zuria Bauer, Hermann Blum, Peter Kontschieder, Marc Pollefeys
- Affiliations: Not provided
- Tier: Worth skimming
- Lane: Graphics / 3D / 4D
- Review depth: Abstract
- Estimated reading time: 4 minutes

### Abstract

Map-Det3D repurposes a feed-forward metric 3D reconstruction model as the geometric backbone for online object detection from a short RGB video window. It predicts boxes directly in reconstructed metric space instead of detecting in 2D and lifting uncertain depth and scale afterward.

### Editorial note

Map-Det3D moves monocular video detection into metric space by tuning a feed-forward reconstruction prior and predicting 3D boxes directly, avoiding the brittle 2D-detect-then-lift pattern. The ECCV 2026 abstract reports strong online performance and transfer without adaptation, and code and models are linked. Exact benchmark gains and compute cost require paper-level inspection.

## How Can Driving World Models Do Counterfactual Prediction?

- Authors: Jiaru Zhang, Can Cui, Yi Xu, Xin Ye, Ruqi Zhang, Ziran Wang
- Affiliations: Not provided
- Tier: Worth skimming
- Lane: Broader vision
- Review depth: Abstract
- Estimated reading time: 4 minutes

### Abstract

This work argues that action-conditioned driving prediction is not automatically counterfactual because it ignores the factual continuation needed for causal abduction. A controlled benchmark and a training-free evidence-transfer pipeline test how much episode-specific information can be preserved under an alternative ego action.

### Editorial note

This paper identifies a useful causal mismatch: conditioning a driving world model on an alternative action does not abduct episode-specific facts from the observed continuation, so a plausible future may not be the matched counterfactual. A controlled simulator benchmark shows the gap, and a simple training-free evidence-transfer construction improves recovered content and perceptual distance. The analysis assumes a short horizon in which surrounding agents do not react to the changed ego action, so its clean diagnosis is not yet the general interactive case.

## From Synthesis to Removal: Physics-Grounded Reflection Simulation and Diffusion-Based Video Dereflection

- Authors: Zepeng Wang, Jiagao Hu, Fuhao Li, Yuxuan Chen, Fei Wang, Daiguo Zhou
- Affiliations: Not provided
- Tier: Worth skimming
- Lane: Broader vision
- Review depth: Abstract
- Estimated reading time: 4 minutes

### Abstract

S2R closes the video-dereflection data loop with physics-grounded synthesis, a diffusion removal model, and a dedicated benchmark. Structure-space augmentation models blur, ghosting, and reflectance variation, while the removal model adapts video diffusion and adds one-step pixel-geometric refinement.

### Editorial note

S2R is an unusually complete treatment of video reflections: physics-grounded synthesis creates paired data, a one-step diffusion-based remover targets temporal consistency, and a new benchmark covers full-reference and real-world human evaluation. The abstract reports state-of-the-art results and inference faster than non-diffusion baselines, but the benchmark and training data are introduced together, making external generalization the key question.

## AI4AI at Test-Time: Strong-to-Weak Capability Transfer via Harnesses

- Authors: Cheng Qian, Wenting Zhao, Liangwei Yang, Heng Wang, Jielin Qiu, Heng Ji, Silvio Savarese, Huan Wang, Shelby Heinecke
- Affiliations: Not provided
- Tier: Worth skimming
- Lane: General AI
- Review depth: Abstract
- Estimated reading time: 4 minutes

### Abstract

AI4AI studies whether a stronger model can transfer capability to a weaker model at inference time by building a task harness rather than updating model weights. The harnesses move unstable reasoning into deterministic code, routing, and answer-format enforcement.

### Editorial note

AI4AI asks whether a strong model can transfer capability at test time by constructing a deterministic harness for a weaker model, with no weight updates. Across four Theory-of-Mind benchmarks, average target performance rises from 0.49 to 0.91; the authors attribute most gains to code, benchmark-specific routing, and answer formatting rather than more free-form reasoning. The use of 5% benchmark data to iteratively design each harness makes contamination and task-specific engineering important caveats.
