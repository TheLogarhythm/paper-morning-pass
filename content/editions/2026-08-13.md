## Amulet: Frame Extrapolation Through Sparse Layered Scene Representation and Adaptive Shading

- Authors: Sebastian Künzel, Fabian Schmierer, Sergej Geringer, Guido Reina, Daniel Weiskopf, Dieter Schmalstieg
- Affiliations: Not provided
- Tier: Read first
- Lane: Graphics / 3D / 4D
- Review depth: Abstract
- Estimated reading time: 8 minutes

### Abstract

Amulet accelerates interactive rendering with a sparse, tiled, layered scene cache that stores potentially visible geometry and predictive shading information for future views. An adaptive scheduler distributes shading work while display refresh proceeds at a higher rate, including newly disoccluded regions without relying on neural synthesis.

### Verdict

The day's strongest pure-graphics systems paper: a sparse explicit scene cache raises display rate while preserving visibility information for disocclusions instead of hallucinating them.

### Contribution

Amulet rasterizes potentially visible geometry into a sparse tiled layered representation, caches predictive shading information, and schedules tile updates adaptively so rasterization, shading, and display refresh can run at different rates.

### Why it matters

Frame generation is increasingly neural, but interactive graphics still benefits from methods with explicit geometry, predictable failure modes, and no training dependency. Amulet's representation-level approach is especially relevant to high-refresh and high-resolution rendering.

### Evidence

The abstract reports converting typical 60 Hz shading to 240 Hz display and reaching up to 250 Hz at 4K, while remaining competitive with DLSS and neural-flow baselines on multiple quality metrics.

### Limitation

The performance and quality statements are author-reported abstract results; the full paper is needed to understand scene-dependent memory cost, latency, and failure behavior under rapid camera or content changes.

## Beyond Pixels: From Video Priors to 4D Worlds

- Authors: Zihao Liu, Xiaolong Shen, Zhenglin Zhou, Ruijie Quan, Yi Yang
- Affiliations: Not provided
- Tier: Read first
- Lane: Graphics / 3D / 4D
- Review depth: Abstract
- Estimated reading time: 8 minutes

### Abstract

Latent-to-4D treats the final denoised latent of a video diffusion model as a reusable interface to an explicit 4D decoder. It bypasses RGB reconstruction, aligns video latents with the decoder token grid, and refines them with frame-wise and global spatiotemporal attention.

### Verdict

A clean architectural bridge between video generation and explicit 4D reconstruction: reuse the shared VAE latent rather than repeatedly translating through RGB or retraining for each video backbone.

### Contribution

Latent-to-4D aligns denoised video latents with a pretrained 4D decoder's token grid and refines them with local and global spatiotemporal attention; one checkpoint can transfer among video diffusion transformers that share the same VAE.

### Why it matters

A stable latent interface could make advances in video priors immediately useful for dynamic 3D generation while avoiding cascade error and reducing coupling to a specific generator.

### Evidence

Trained on roughly 1,000 reconstruction clips, the method improves projection-based DINO-F1 over matched Wan-plus-4RC cascades by 2.88–3.45 points on Text4D-200 and 5.81 points on I4D-200; the abstract also reports human preference for geometry, temporal stability, and overall quality.

### Limitation

Transfer is limited to generators sharing a VAE family, and the evidence is reported by the authors on two 200-example benchmarks; broader motion, topology, and camera regimes need inspection.

## Gaussian Sculpting: End-to-End Controllable Surface Reconstruction via Field Optimization

- Authors: Ke Jiaxin, Juncheng Liu, Yi Wang, Zhouhui Lian, Bin Liu, Shengfa Wang, Xiangjia He
- Affiliations: Not provided
- Tier: Read first
- Lane: Graphics / 3D / 4D
- Review depth: Abstract
- Estimated reading time: 8 minutes

### Abstract

Gaussian Sculpting couples 3D Gaussians to an evolving differentiable surface so they guide signed-distance-field optimization rather than merely render the result. Bilevel training, alignment constraints, and multi-resolution subdivision aim to remove redundant surfaces and recover missing structure from limited views.

### Verdict

A compelling representation-coupling idea: Gaussian primitives become optimization instruments for an explicit surface, combining rendering flexibility with a controllable geometric output.

### Contribution

The method anchors Gaussians to an evolving differentiable surface, isolates their respective gradients through bilevel training, aligns the two representations with explicit constraints, and adds detail through multi-resolution subdivision.

### Why it matters

Surface reconstruction from limited views often oscillates between visually effective volumetric primitives and meshes or fields that are easier to edit and simulate. Jointly optimizing both could make the strengths of 3DGS useful for higher-quality explicit geometry.

### Evidence

The abstract reports removing redundant surfaces and recovering missing structure in limited-view object reconstruction, with particularly strong performance at low input resolution.

### Limitation

The abstract provides no numerical comparisons or evidence on large scenes, and joint field–Gaussian optimization may introduce substantial training complexity that requires full-paper evaluation.

## WildFireGS: Physics-Based Wildfire Simulation in Large-Scale Semantics-Enriched Gaussian Splatting Forest Scenes

- Authors: Nienke Driessen, Joris Rijsdijk, Sören Pirk, Wojtek Palubicki, Dominik L. Michels, Michael Weinmann
- Affiliations: Not provided
- Tier: Read first
- Lane: Graphics / 3D / 4D
- Review depth: Abstract
- Estimated reading time: 7 minutes

### Abstract

WildFireGS performs physics-based wildfire simulation directly in large-scale Gaussian-splat forest reconstructions. It augments Gaussian primitives with vegetation, material, and fuel attributes and couples them to particle combustion without first converting the scene to a mesh or voxel grid.

### Verdict

An unusual and useful graphics–simulation intersection: it turns a captured Gaussian forest into a semantically and physically actionable wildfire environment without an intermediate mesh or grid.

### Contribution

WildFireGS attaches vegetation, material, and fuel attributes to Gaussian primitives and runs a particle-based combustion model directly over the enriched reconstruction.

### Why it matters

Gaussian splats are usually treated as appearance representations. Making them carry simulation state could broaden captured-scene workflows toward environmental analysis, planning, and counterfactual experiments.

### Evidence

Across synthetic and aerial forest captures, the abstract reports physically consistent responses to vegetation density, wind, and slope, together with firebreak and biomass-loss experiments.

### Limitation

The abstract does not quantify agreement with measured fires or operational simulators, so 'physics-based' should not yet be interpreted as validated predictive accuracy.

## CausalSplat: Towards Comprehensive Hierarchical Reasoning in 3D Gaussian Splatting

- Authors: Jiayu Ding, Meilu Song, Yun Chen, Wei Gao, Ge Li
- Affiliations: Not provided
- Tier: Read first
- Lane: Graphics / 3D / 4D
- Review depth: Abstract
- Estimated reading time: 7 minutes

### Abstract

CausalSplat combines 3D Gaussian scene representations, vision-language models, and scene graphs to support hierarchical reasoning over 3D environments. The work introduces benchmarks spanning commonsense, spatial, affordance, and counterfactual questions in addition to evaluating referring and open-vocabulary segmentation.

### Verdict

A notable step from labeling Gaussian scenes toward asking structured spatial, functional, and counterfactual questions about them, backed by purpose-built 3D reasoning benchmarks.

### Contribution

CausalSplat integrates vision-language reasoning with 3D Gaussian scene graphs and introduces Causal-LERF and Causal-ScanNet to evaluate commonsense, spatial, affordance, and counterfactual reasoning.

### Why it matters

If Gaussian scenes are to support agents and editing systems, they need queryable semantics that extend beyond open-vocabulary masks. Hierarchical reasoning is a natural bridge between scene representation and action.

### Evidence

The ECCV 2026 abstract reports state-of-the-art performance on its new reasoning benchmarks and generalization gains on referring and open-vocabulary 3D segmentation.

### Limitation

The benchmarks are introduced by the same work and exact baselines and margins are absent from the abstract; independent robustness and benchmark leakage need full-paper scrutiny.

## Self-Geometry: GT-Free and Plug-and-Play Test-Time Adaptation for Geometrically Consistent 3D Vision Foundation Models

- Authors: Seokhyun Youn, Dahyeon Kye, Sung-Ho Bae, Jihyong Oh
- Affiliations: Not provided
- Tier: Worth skimming
- Lane: Graphics / 3D / 4D
- Review depth: Abstract
- Estimated reading time: 4 minutes

### Abstract

Self-Geometry adapts 3D vision foundation models at test time using multiview pixel correspondences as geometric pseudo-labels. Its consistency objectives and gradient disentanglement are designed to improve geometry without ground-truth depth or camera annotations, using lightweight LoRA updates.

### Editorial note

Self-Geometry uses explicit cross-view correspondences as pseudo-ground truth for plug-and-play test-time adaptation of 3D vision foundation models. Its geometric disentanglement, multiview and epipolar consistency, SO(3)-aware sampling, and LoRA updates improve six model configurations across four benchmarks according to the abstract. It is worth skimming as a broadly reusable adaptation recipe, though online optimization cost and failure under bad correspondences need closer inspection.

## Compact Feed-Forward 3D Gaussians via Saliency-Guided Primitive Merging

- Authors: Tim-Felix Fassch, Jochen Kall, Cyrill Stachniss
- Affiliations: Not provided
- Tier: Worth skimming
- Lane: Graphics / 3D / 4D
- Review depth: Abstract
- Estimated reading time: 4 minutes

### Abstract

This backbone-agnostic postprocessor compresses feed-forward 3D Gaussian predictions by clustering coherent primitives with saliency-guided adaptive superpixels, encoding them, and merging them across views. A level-of-detail decoder supports adjustable reconstruction budgets.

### Editorial note

This postprocessor compresses feed-forward 3DGS predictions through saliency-aware spatial grouping, learned encoding, cross-view merging, and level-of-detail decoding. The headline result—retaining reconstruction quality with one twentieth of the Gaussian primitives—is practically significant and backbone-agnostic. The abstract does not expose runtime, memory, or thin-structure tradeoffs, so the main value of a skim is to inspect what survives aggressive merging.

## The GENEA Challenge 2026: A Large-Scale Disentangled Evaluation of Speech-Driven Gesture Generation on the Seamless Interaction Dataset

- Authors: Rajmund Nagy, Silvia Arellano García, Hendric Voss, Mihail Tsakov, Taras Kucherenko, Youngwoo Yoon, Gustav Eje Henter
- Affiliations: Not provided
- Tier: Worth skimming
- Lane: Graphics / 3D / 4D
- Review depth: Abstract
- Estimated reading time: 4 minutes

### Abstract

GENEA Challenge 2026 evaluates speech-driven gesture generation through four large user studies that separate motion quality, speech alignment, dyadic interaction, and semantic appropriateness. The results expose substantial remaining gaps to motion-capture references, particularly for interaction and meaning.

### Editorial note

GENEA 2026 is valuable less as another gesture model than as a large human-evaluation snapshot: four studies collected more than 23,000 votes from 869 participants while separating motion quality, speech alignment, dyadic interaction, and semantics. Motion quality is approaching reference capture for some systems, but alignment and especially dyadic and semantic behavior remain far behind. Public outputs and votes should make this a useful benchmark audit resource.

## R4DSG: Relative 4D Scene Graph Memory for Object-Centric Question Answering in Long Egocentric Video

- Authors: Ke Ma, Yamin Mao, Weiming Li, Shuai Tan, Yijie Zhong, Hao Chen, Haofen Wang, Meng Wang
- Affiliations: Not provided
- Tier: Worth skimming
- Lane: Broader vision
- Review depth: Abstract
- Estimated reading time: 4 minutes

### Abstract

R4DSG represents long egocentric video as compact memory entries indexed by time, place, persistent objects, anchor-relative change, and interaction context. Stable anchors and relative transitions avoid requiring a globally aligned reconstruction while preserving object identity for question answering.

### Editorial note

R4DSG stores persistent objects, stable anchors, relative changes, time, place, and interactions as compact queryable memory rather than demanding a globally aligned 3D reconstruction of free-motion egocentric video. On a 255-question EgoLifeQA subset, question-only retrieval improves 6.7 points overall and 12.5 points on 'when' questions over EgoRAG-Text. The object-memory design is the interesting part; broader validation beyond this subset remains open.
