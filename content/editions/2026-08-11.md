## R2S-EGO: Dual-Proxy Refinement for Sparse-Capture Real-to-Sim

- Authors: Shuai Fang, Xin Deng, Yuchen Kang, Zhenjiang Li, Jie Chen
- Affiliations: XPENG Robotics, The Hong Kong Polytechnic University
- Tier: Read first
- Lane: Graphics / 3D / 4D
- Review depth: Full paper
- Estimated reading time: 8 minutes

### Abstract

R2S-EGO refines sparsely captured real-to-sim scenes around the camera views an embodied robot will actually use. It couples a simulator-derived robot proxy for executable ego-view queries with capture-anchored geometry for structurally conditioned view synthesis, then folds registered generated observations back into the visual asset and collision proxy.

### Verdict

A strong graphics-to-robotics bridge that frames missing robot-camera support as targeted scene refinement rather than indiscriminate reconstruction densification.

Primary source: [source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06827).

### Contribution

The method couples executable robot-view queries with capture-anchored geometry, selects structurally supported deficits, generates registered ego observations, and feeds them back into both the 3DGS asset and collision proxy.

Primary source: [source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06827).

### Why it matters

It connects sparse-view rendering quality to a downstream embodied-control outcome and offers a concrete design for spending generative reconstruction budget where a robot will consume it.

Primary source: [source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06827).

### Evidence

With six real views, the paper reports 19.062 dB PSNR versus 14.226 dB for its strongest reported real-to-sim baseline across 48 held-out ego views, plus 33 of 40 successful real-G1 sitting trials versus 4 of 40 for GaussGym.

Primary source: [source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06827).

### Limitation

The controlled evaluation covers three Replica scenes, one robot embodiment and sitting behavior, and does not independently validate the metric or collision accuracy of prior-completed geometry.

Primary source: [source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06827).

## InstanceSplat: Instance-Aware Feed-Forward 3D Gaussian Splatting for Scene Understanding

- Authors: Minchao Jiang, Xiaoxuan Ma, Shunyu Jia, Haoru Wang, Zhang Liang, Wentao Zhu
- Affiliations: Not provided
- Tier: Read first
- Lane: Graphics / 3D / 4D
- Review depth: Full paper
- Estimated reading time: 8 minutes

### Abstract

InstanceSplat is a pose-free feed-forward 3D Gaussian Splatting system that jointly represents appearance, geometry, instance identity, and language-aligned semantics. Its shared Gaussians ground instances across views, while instance-centric coupling lets reconstruction and semantic understanding improve one another.

### Verdict

A particularly relevant unification of feed-forward 3DGS reconstruction with instance-level and open-vocabulary scene understanding.

Primary source: [source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07144).

### Contribution

InstanceSplat predicts pose-free multi-view Gaussians carrying appearance, geometry, instance identity, and language-aligned semantics, then couples instance structure back into boundaries and semantic aggregation.

Primary source: [source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07144).

### Why it matters

The work treats rendering and recognition as mutually useful signals in a single explicit 3D representation, which is more interesting than simply attaching semantic features after reconstruction.

Primary source: [source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07144).

### Evidence

On ScanNet, the paper reports the best PSNR and LPIPS among reconstruction-reporting feed-forward methods and the highest mIoU among non-ablation methods across 2, 4, 8, and 16 input views, with additional unseen-dataset evaluation.

Primary source: [source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07144).

### Limitation

The authors identify feature clustering cost as a scaling bottleneck as the number of views and Gaussians grows.

Primary source: [source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07144).

## Scenix: Sparse-View 3D Scene Reconstruction via Executable Scene Programs

- Authors: Kai Li, Lutao Jiang, Zhenyang Li, Jiayu Dong, Jierui Zhang, Yingda Yin, Runze Zhang, Kai Yan, Xiaoyang Huang, Keyang Luo, Xin Wang, Xiangyu Zhao, Weikai Chen
- Affiliations: Not provided
- Tier: Read first
- Lane: Graphics / 3D / 4D
- Review depth: Full paper
- Estimated reading time: 8 minutes

### Abstract

Scenix reconstructs editable indoor scenes from a few uncalibrated RGB views by predicting executable scene programs rather than a monolithic field. It combines structured scene induction, perception-grounded asset instantiation, and a critic-editor-verify refinement loop, supported by the roughly 110,000-scene XScene dataset.

### Verdict

Executable scene programs are a compelling intermediate representation for turning sparse visual observations into structured, editable 3D scenes.

Primary source: [source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07012).

### Contribution

Scenix predicts room and object programs from uncalibrated views, grounds generated assets in observations, and closes the loop with critic-editor-verifier spatial refinement; XScene supplies roughly 110,000 supervised cases.

Primary source: [source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07012).

### Why it matters

The representation separates perception, layout, and asset realization, preserving object-level editability while addressing the correspondence and global-consistency problems of sparse scene capture.

Primary source: [source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07012).

### Evidence

The paper evaluates held-out synthetic and real XScene cases, single-view baselines, and zero-shot SpatialGen transfer, reporting stronger layout recovery and consistent gains from grounded residual refinement.

Primary source: [source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07012).

### Limitation

About 91 percent of XScene training cases are synthetic, while the real test subset is 242 single-view SUN RGB-D cases, so real sparse multi-view generalization remains less directly established.

Primary source: [source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07012).

## Flow-Corrected Shape Optimization: Taming Manifold Drift in High-Dimensional 3D Models

- Authors: Emilien Seiler, Nicolas Talabot, Yingxuan You, Federico Stella, Pascal Fua
- Affiliations: Not provided
- Tier: Worth skimming
- Lane: Graphics / 3D / 4D
- Review depth: Abstract
- Estimated reading time: 4 minutes

### Abstract

This work targets manifold drift during gradient-based optimization in high-dimensional 3D generative latents. It alternates free objective-minimization steps with guided flow-matching corrections that return the state toward the learned shape manifold, and evaluates the design on engineering objectives including drag and compliance.

### Editorial note

The optimizer-corrector split is a clean response to latent-manifold drift in high-dimensional 3D generators: optimize the engineering objective freely, then use guided flow matching to restore shape validity. The reported drag and compliance examples make it relevant to graphics-adjacent geometry processing and computer-aided engineering, although the abstract alone does not establish how broadly the correction scales.

Primary source: [source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07199).

## C2Dex: Contact-Consistent Reconstruction and Retargeting for Dexterous Manipulation from Monocular Video

- Authors: Jie Ren, Zhehao Jiang, Yinhong Yang, Haorui Jia, Han Jiang, Ben Li, Yao Yao, Cheng Lin, Qiu Shen, Zhenshan Bing, Xiao-Xiao Long, Xun Cao
- Affiliations: Not provided
- Tier: Worth skimming
- Lane: Graphics / 3D / 4D
- Review depth: Abstract
- Estimated reading time: 4 minutes

### Abstract

C2Dex turns monocular human manipulation video into dexterous-robot trajectories through stable object-side contacts aggregated in canonical object space. Those contacts constrain human hand-object reconstruction and provide transfer targets for geometry-preserving retargeting, followed by simulation refinement.

### Editorial note

C2Dex makes object-side contacts the shared currency between monocular hand-object reconstruction and robot-hand retargeting, a sensible way to preserve task geometry across embodiments. The abstract reports end-to-end success of 57.78 percent on DexYCB and 26.67 percent on TACO versus 17.78 and 10.00 percent for the strongest baselines, with real-robot replays as a physical-feasibility check. Its value is at the vision, geometry, and manipulation boundary rather than as a general reconstruction method.

Primary source: [source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07045).

## Addressable Memory for Video World Models

- Authors: Xindi Wu, Sven Elflein, James Lucas, Olga Russakovsky, Laura Leal-Taixé, Despoina Paschalidou, Jonathan Lorraine, Aljoša Ošep
- Affiliations: Not provided
- Tier: Worth skimming
- Lane: Broader vision
- Review depth: Abstract
- Estimated reading time: 4 minutes

### Abstract

WorldTrace is a training-free memory framework for interactive video world models whose temporal positional encodings otherwise drift beyond the training horizon. It assigns compressed summaries in-distribution virtual positions and separates temporal field compression from landmark-like episodic traces, with a LoopBench evaluation for scene revisitation.

### Editorial note

WorldTrace identifies a concrete long-horizon failure in video world models: cache content becomes difficult to address when temporal RoPE offsets move out of the training range, and naive compression mixes incompatible phases. Its training-free virtual-position memory reports 15.5 percent better temporal consistency and 19.5 percent better episodic recall on LoopBench. This is worth tracking as a systems idea for persistent visual generation, though the benchmark is introduced by the same work.

Primary source: [source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07408).

## SubtleTalk: Generating Controllable Weakly-correlated Facial Dynamics for 3D Talking Heads via Residual Flow Matching

- Authors: Chenyang Ding, Shuai Tan, Qunfen Lin, Xinwei Jiang, Zijiao Zeng, Ye Pan
- Affiliations: Not provided
- Tier: Worth skimming
- Lane: Graphics / 3D / 4D
- Review depth: Abstract
- Estimated reading time: 4 minutes

### Abstract

SubtleTalk models upper-face, blink, and head dynamics that are only weakly tied to speech in 3D talking heads. It adds interpretable prosody, regional-intensity, and affect controls to a speech-driven prior, then uses residual flow matching to generate diverse deviations, supported by a 74-hour facial-animation dataset.

### Editorial note

SubtleTalk focuses on the facial motions that lip-sync objectives underspecify, combining interpretable affect and intensity controls with residual flow matching around a stable speech-driven prior. The accompanying SubtleTalk-Face dataset covers about 3,900 identities and 74 hours, which may be as useful to follow as the model itself. The abstract claims better realism and diversity while retaining lip synchronization, but full metric and generalization details need paper-level inspection.

Primary source: [source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06408).

## CANIS: Generation-Assisted 3D Canonicalization via an Image-Semantic Bridge

- Authors: Kendong Liu, Yuxin Yao, Junhui Hou
- Affiliations: Not provided
- Tier: Worth skimming
- Lane: Graphics / 3D / 4D
- Review depth: Abstract
- Estimated reading time: 4 minutes

### Abstract

CANIS transfers semantic orientation priors from a frozen image-to-3D generator into category-agnostic 3D object canonicalization. It creates a canonical proxy, uses rendered image patches as a semantic bridge, back-projects semantic anchors, and estimates a rigid alignment without canonicalization-specific training or templates.

### Editorial note

CANIS uses a frozen image-to-3D model as a semantic orientation prior, then bridges generated canonical proxies back to input geometry through image-patch anchors and depth back-projection. The category-agnostic, no-task-training setup is appealing for shape pipelines that need consistent coordinates. Evidence is strongest on synthetic benchmarks, while results on partial and real scans are described qualitatively, so it remains a promising idea rather than a settled solution.

Primary source: [source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07256).
