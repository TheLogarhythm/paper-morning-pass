## Differentiate the Solver, Not the Equation: Reverse-Sweep Adjoints for Block Implicit Simulation

- Authors: Lei Shu, Ying Jiang, Kui Wu, Yin Yang, Leonidas Guibas, Chenfanfu Jiang
- Affiliations: Not provided
- Tier: Read first
- Lane: Graphics / 3D / 4D
- Review depth: Abstract
- Estimated reading time: 8 minutes

### Abstract

This work differentiates the finite block-implicit solver that actually runs rather than differentiating only its converged equation. Reversing the sequence of local implicit updates yields a solver-level adjoint whose backward pass mirrors the locality of the forward solver and avoids a global adjoint system.

### Verdict

The day's clearest graphics-method contribution: it preserves the executed implicit solver's local structure in the adjoint instead of replacing it with a global equation-level differentiation problem.

Primary source: [source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.08559).

### Contribution

For block implicit updates, the method reverses the forward update order and solves only local adjoint blocks; its Vertex Block Descent instance uses local 3-by-3 solves and builds no global adjoint system.

Primary source: [source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.08559).

### Why it matters

Differentiable simulation often becomes impractical because unrolling consumes memory and global implicit differentiation discards solver locality. A solver-mirroring backward pass could make inverse design and learning through large contact-rich simulations substantially more usable.

Primary source: [source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.08559).

### Evidence

The abstract reports machine-precision agreement with automatic differentiation of the same finite forward solve, 33x speed and 71x memory improvements over unrolled differentiation in a controlled comparison, and a one-GPU demonstration with one million contact-coupled soft bodies.

Primary source: [source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.08559).

### Limitation

These are author-reported abstract results for a block-implicit solver family; the abstract does not establish how directly the construction transfers to substantially different nonlinear solvers or hardware regimes.

Primary source: [source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.08559).

## A Hybrid Neural-Microfacet BRDF Model for Real-Time Rendering

- Authors: Louis De Oliveira, Anastasia Karpova, Georges Nader, Antoine Houdard, Pierre Mezieres, Damien Rioux-Lavoie, Romain Pacanowski
- Affiliations: Not provided
- Tier: Read first
- Lane: Graphics / 3D / 4D
- Review depth: Abstract
- Estimated reading time: 7 minutes

### Abstract

This EGSR 2026 paper combines an editable GGX-style microfacet model with a compact neural residual that corrects appearance effects the analytic component misses. The hybrid design targets measured or simulated BRDF fidelity while retaining importance sampling and low enough overhead for real-time rendering.

### Verdict

A pragmatic rendering paper that uses an analytic BRDF for structure and editability while reserving a small network for the residual appearance that microfacet models miss.

Primary source: [source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09604).

### Contribution

The hybrid representation augments a GGX-type microfacet base with a neural correction, retaining artist controls and importance sampling while reducing the network size needed to approximate measured, simulated, or procedural reflectance.

Primary source: [source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09604).

### Why it matters

Pure neural BRDFs can be expensive and hard to edit, while specialized analytic models lose generality. The residual formulation offers a potentially deployable middle ground for real-time material appearance.

Primary source: [source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09604).

### Evidence

The EGSR 2026 abstract reports better approximation of measured BRDF data than prior neural models at equal memory cost, with low evaluation overhead relative to a microfacet model and an available project page.

Primary source: [source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09604).

### Limitation

The abstract does not quantify the runtime overhead or show how fidelity changes across the full range of difficult optical effects, so deployment tradeoffs require paper-level inspection.

Primary source: [source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09604).

## FlexSplat: Flexible Feed-Forward 3D Gaussian Splatting without Point Cloud Correspondence

- Authors: Amir Sabbaghziarani, Hanting Ye, Maria Gorlatova, Yi Ding
- Affiliations: Not provided
- Tier: Read first
- Lane: Graphics / 3D / 4D
- Review depth: Abstract
- Estimated reading time: 7 minutes

### Abstract

FlexSplat reconstructs an object-centric Gaussian representation from uncalibrated multi-view images by jointly predicting cameras and depth, then aggregating evidence into a compact set of query-based 3D Gaussians. Its primitive budget is decoupled from image resolution and input-view count, avoiding pixel-aligned point-cloud correspondence.

### Verdict

A strong calibration-free extension of query-based feed-forward 3DGS, especially relevant because it decouples the Gaussian budget from pixels, point correspondences, and view count.

Primary source: [source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07937).

### Contribution

FlexSplat jointly predicts cameras and depth, uses them to ground a compact Gaussian parameterization, and aggregates multi-view evidence through depth-guided deformable attention with uncertainty-weighted depth consistency.

Primary source: [source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07937).

### Why it matters

Removing known-camera and point-cloud assumptions makes feed-forward Gaussian reconstruction more useful for ordinary image collections, while a fixed compact primitive budget addresses a practical scaling weakness of pixel-aligned approaches.

Primary source: [source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07937).

### Evidence

The BMVC 2026 abstract reports performance within 0.7 dB PSNR of posed methods and matching best perceptual quality on Google Scanned Objects despite using neither ground-truth camera poses nor depth; code is linked from arXiv.

Primary source: [source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07937).

### Limitation

The reported evaluation is object-centric on ShapeNet-SRN and Google Scanned Objects, so the abstract does not establish behavior on large, cluttered, or non-rigid scenes.

Primary source: [source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07937).

## PhysX-CoT: Structured Physical Reasoning from a Single Image to Simulation-Ready 3D Assets

- Authors: Jie Huang, Xiaohe Li, Jiahao Li, Fangli Mou, Chen Qian, Yuqiang Fang, Junhao Fan, Kaixin Zhang, Zide Fan
- Affiliations: Not provided
- Tier: Read first
- Lane: Graphics / 3D / 4D
- Review depth: Abstract
- Estimated reading time: 7 minutes

### Abstract

PhysX-CoT exposes single-image 3D asset generation as a supervised, machine-parseable sequence of part decomposition, grounding, relations, geometry, and surface cues. It factorizes placement into 3D boxes and local shape codes, then aligns reinforcement-learning rewards with parse validity, geometry, and physical consistency.

### Verdict

A promising attempt to make image-to-3D assets inspectably physical by exposing intermediate part states rather than asking a model to emit one opaque serialized asset.

Primary source: [source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.08053).

### Contribution

PhysX-CoT supervises an ordered trajectory of part decomposition, 2D/3D grounding, relations, coarse geometry, and surface cues, then uses those states both to condition geometry and to define reinforcement-learning rewards.

Primary source: [source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.08053).

### Why it matters

Simulation-ready assets need more than visual resemblance: their parts must be placed, parsed, collided, and articulated coherently. Explicit intermediate states provide handles for training, verification, and later editing.

Primary source: [source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.08053).

### Evidence

Under a protocol that retrains learned baselines on the same backbone, data, and frozen decoder, the abstract reports gains across geometry, scale, and physical-attribute metrics, plus high parse, collision, and articulation validity in Unreal Engine 5.

Primary source: [source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.08053).

### Limitation

The evidence is tied to a shared frozen decoder and the abstract does not report downstream robot-policy gains, so simulation validity should not yet be read as task-level effectiveness.

Primary source: [source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.08053).

- [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.08053)

### Abstract

Uni4R learns a continuous velocity field shared by 4D reconstruction and point tracking. Optimal-transport flow matching supplies a global kinematic prior, while ODE integration turns integer-timestamp supervision into predictions at arbitrary intermediate times.

## NewtonGS: Physics-Structured Object-Level Neural Newtonian Dynamics for Gaussian Scene Animation

- Authors: Lianlei Shan, Feiyang Ye, Yan Chen, Yong Wu
- Affiliations: Not provided
- Tier: Worth skimming
- Lane: Graphics / 3D / 4D
- Review depth: Abstract
- Estimated reading time: 4 minutes

### Abstract

NewtonGS gives objects in static Gaussian scenes an explicit 22-dimensional physical state and rolls that state forward with analytic Newtonian structure plus learned continuous and contact residuals. Predicted poses and scales then update each object's Gaussian means and covariances through a shared affine transform.

### Editorial note

NewtonGS gives each Gaussian-scene object an explicit Newtonian state and combines analytic translation, quaternion kinematics, gravity, damping, and contact handling with learned residuals. That is a useful control-oriented alternative to merely reconstructing time-varying Gaussians. The reported evidence comes from two procedurally generated 32-object datasets, so real-scene generalization remains the main question.

Primary source: [source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07598).

## Sci-VBench: Evaluating Knowledge- and Reasoning-Intensive Video Generation in Science Domains

- Authors: Diandian Zhang, Tingyu Song, Lin Fu, Zheyuan Yang, Yilun Zhao
- Affiliations: Not provided
- Tier: Worth skimming
- Lane: Broader vision
- Review depth: Abstract
- Estimated reading time: 4 minutes

### Abstract

Sci-VBench evaluates whether generated videos preserve scientific and causal correctness rather than only visual plausibility. It supplies 1,253 expert-annotated prompts across 60 subjects, a rubric-based evaluation protocol, and results from 16 proprietary and open video-generation models.

### Editorial note

Sci-VBench asks whether generated videos obey scientific and causal constraints, not just whether frames look plausible. Its 1,253 expert-annotated examples span 60 subjects, and its evaluation of 16 models finds tightly clustered perceptual scores but large differences in grounding and scientific correctness. It is worth tracking as evidence that video-generation evaluation needs domain reasoning, though the benchmark's own rubric and judge calibration merit close inspection.

Primary source: [source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09873).

## On-Policy Self-Distillation without Any Supervision

- Authors: Yijiang Li, Bingyang Wang, Yijun Liang, Yunjie Tian, Di Fu, Nuno Vasconcelos
- Affiliations: Not provided
- Tier: Worth skimming
- Lane: General AI
- Review depth: Abstract
- Estimated reading time: 4 minutes

### Abstract

U-OPSD constructs pseudo-solutions from self-consistent model rollouts and distills the model specifically on disagreeing completions, avoiding ground-truth labels, environment rewards, or a larger teacher. The reported experiments focus on mathematical reasoning with Qwen3 models at 4B and 8B scales.

### Editorial note

U-OPSD turns agreement among a model's own sampled solutions into a pseudo-target and distills only the disagreeing trajectories, removing explicit labels, environment feedback, and a larger teacher. On five math benchmarks, the abstract reports average non-thinking-mode gains of 8.5 and 10.7 percent over Qwen3 4B and 8B bases, with code available. The evaluation is concentrated on mathematical reasoning, so transfer to broader language or multimodal tasks remains open.

Primary source: [source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06296).

## When Does An Extra View Help? Adapting Single-View 3D Reconstruction with Extra Imagery

- Authors: Y Huynh, Duc Thanh Nguyen, Thao Minh Le, Mohamed Abdelrazek
- Affiliations: Not provided
- Tier: Worth skimming
- Lane: Graphics / 3D / 4D
- Review depth: Abstract
- Estimated reading time: 4 minutes

### Abstract

ASV3D adapts existing single-view 3D reconstruction systems at test time using one additional unconstrained image. It offers a zero-shot adaptation route and a contrastive optimized route intended to improve fidelity and cross-view consistency without redesigning the base reconstruction pipeline.

### Editorial note

ASV3D is a practical test-time adaptation wrapper: give a single-view reconstructor one extra unconstrained image, then use either zero-shot adaptation or a contrastive optimized variant to improve fidelity and cross-view consistency. The abstract reports gains across two base pipelines, benchmark and real-world data, plus released code and a real-object dataset. Its appeal is compatibility with existing single-view systems rather than a new reconstruction backbone.

Primary source: [source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.08132).
