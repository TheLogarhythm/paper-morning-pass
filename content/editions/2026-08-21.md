## MegaParts: Scaling Part-Aware 3D Object Generation to 300 Parts via Token-Efficient Autoregressive Modeling

- Authors: Manwen Liao, Xinyu Lian, Jian Mao, Kaixu Chen, Li Luo, Jinghao Yan, Wanshui Gan, Qiao Yu, Weitian Zhang, Chunhua Shen, Guang Chen, Bo Dai, Xudong Xu, Zhaoyang Lyu
- Affiliations: Not provided
- Tier: Read first
- Lane: Graphics / 3D / 4D
- Review depth: Full paper
- Estimated reading time: 8 minutes

### Abstract

MegaParts combines an adaptive-length vector-quantized part tokenizer with long-context autoregressive modeling to generate structured objects containing as many as 300 parts and sequences as long as 256k tokens.

### Verdict

The standout graphics paper in this catch-up: it treats token efficiency as the key systems constraint for scaling editable part-aware object generation from a few components to genuinely complex assemblies.

### Contribution

MegaParts learns adaptive-length discrete tokens for individual part geometry, then autoregressively emits object bounds, part bounds, and shape tokens in one structured sequence, using long-context training to reach 300 parts and 256k tokens.

### Why it matters

Part structure is useful only if generation remains controllable at realistic object complexity. Compressing simple pieces while allocating more tokens to difficult geometry is a promising bridge between semantic assembly and high-fidelity mesh generation.

### Evidence

On PartObjaverse-Tiny, the paper reports FID 43.40 versus 54.81 for TRELLIS-text and 55.58 for Cube, with CLIP similarity 0.27. Under bounding-box conditioning, part Chamfer distance is 3.01e-2 versus 8.59 for FullPart and 8.01 for XPart, alongside part IoU 0.63 and bounding-box IoU 0.94.

### Limitation

The method is computationally heavy at training and inference time and currently generates geometry without textures or materials. It also does not model semantic labels, physical or functional relationships between parts, and relies partly on curated private data.

## HiFi-BRep: High-Fidelity Latent Representation for Robust B-Rep Generation

- Authors: Junhao Hou, Chenqi Luo, Pufan Wang, Jiaying Lu, Yusheng Liu, Feiwei Qin, Meie Fang, Kun Zhou
- Affiliations: Not provided
- Tier: Read first
- Lane: Graphics / 3D / 4D
- Review depth: Full paper
- Estimated reading time: 8 minutes

### Abstract

HiFi-BRep uses topology-aware encoding and a parallel geometry-topology decoder with differentiable manifold constraints to improve the structural validity of generated boundary representations.

### Verdict

A strong CAD-generation paper whose most useful idea is to make geometric and topological validity part of the learned representation and decoder rather than a fragile post-processing cascade.

### Contribution

A topology-aware encoder removes padding and prevents cross-entity feature contamination, while a single-stage decoder predicts faces, edges, vertices, and their incidence in parallel under differentiable manifold constraints.

### Why it matters

B-Rep generation must produce compilable, manifold solids rather than merely plausible point or surface samples. The paper directly addresses the structural failures that often prevent neural CAD outputs from entering downstream modeling tools.

### Evidence

On DeepCAD, HiFi-BRep reports 72.20% valid solids versus 43.20% for DTGBrepGen, with 90.38% compilability versus 92.48%. On ABC it reaches 32.66% validity versus 24.88%, although its 35.61% compilability trails the baseline's 50.55%, showing both the gain and the remaining domain sensitivity.

### Limitation

Failures still include inconsistent trimming, missing surface patches, T-junctions, non-manifold edges, and degenerate or sliver geometry. The ABC compilability result also shows that stronger validity on one benchmark does not imply uniformly better CAD robustness.

## aDSL: Agentic 3D Creation via Joint Agent-Program Design

- Authors: Rui-Huan Wang, Si-Tong Wei, Jia-Qi He, Heng-Yi Wei, Baoquan Chen, Peng-Shuai Wang
- Affiliations: Not provided
- Tier: Read first
- Lane: Graphics / 3D / 4D
- Review depth: Full paper
- Estimated reading time: 8 minutes

### Abstract

aDSL co-designs a relational 3D domain-specific language and a Plan-Execute-Critic multi-agent workflow so language models can build editable geometry without relying on brittle absolute coordinates.

### Verdict

A persuasive example of interface-model co-design: instead of asking an LLM to become better at fragile geometric bookkeeping, it gives the model a language organized around the spatial relations it can reason about.

### Contribution

The aDSL language exposes composable relational operators, while specialized planning, execution, and critic agents iteratively synthesize and repair programs using rendered and execution feedback without task-specific training.

### Why it matters

Editable programmatic 3D assets offer much better control than opaque generated meshes, but only if agents can produce stable programs. Designing the representation around semantic structure may be more scalable than endlessly patching general-purpose code generation.

### Evidence

Across 200 text prompts and 100 reference shapes, aDSL reports 100% execution success. On ShapeNet it reaches CLIP 29.63 and VQA 65.34; on ABO, 30.39 and 68.10; and on Objaverse, 29.07 and 69.37, outperforming the reported code-generation baselines across these settings.

### Limitation

Complex appearance and material effects remain outside the DSL's expressive range, and a 2D visual critic can misread 3D relations. Runs average roughly 187-195 seconds per round, 4.25-5.23 rounds, and 30-33k input tokens per round, while depending on proprietary LLMs.

## Marionette: Predicting World States, Rendering Geometry, Painting Appearance

- Authors: Zian Meng, Zhen Li, Chuanhao Li, Qiang Li, Kaipeng Zhang
- Affiliations: Not provided
- Tier: Read first
- Lane: Graphics / 3D / 4D
- Review depth: Full paper
- Estimated reading time: 8 minutes

### Abstract

Marionette predicts an explicit 276-dimensional 3D world state, converts it to pose and occlusion controls with a fixed graphics bridge, and uses video diffusion only for appearance synthesis.

### Verdict

A clear architectural argument for explicit world state: geometry and interaction constraints become inspectable and repairable, while the diffusion model is relieved of maintaining them implicitly over long rollouts.

### Contribution

Marionette predicts articulated skeletons, metric trajectories, and rotations in a 276-dimensional state; a fixed renderer derives pose and occlusion controls; a video diffusion model then paints appearance conditioned on those controls.

### Why it matters

World models become more useful for graphics and games when behavior can be edited by changing state rather than prompting pixels. The separation also makes physical rule interventions testable without retraining the appearance model.

### Evidence

Mismatching the action stream changes root-aligned joint error by 31% across 48 held-out segments. State-space terrain and separation rules cut ground penetration by 66% and prevent two characters from drifting to 21.2 meters apart. Reported FVD is 831, versus 975 for the pixel-autoregressive comparison.

### Limitation

Identity and appearance can drift because the explicit state does not fully condition character identity, and unrecorded entities may appear without a corresponding state. Evidence is currently concentrated on a narrow game-character domain.

## GS-Voxel: Fitting-Free Structured Latents for Large-Scale 3DGS Generation

- Authors: Ming Qian, Zijian Wang, Minchao Sun, Jincheng Xiong, Hang Zhang, Mu Xu, Chi Wang, Baoquan Chen
- Affiliations: Not provided
- Tier: Read first
- Lane: Graphics / 3D / 4D
- Review depth: Abstract
- Estimated reading time: 7 minutes

### Abstract

GS-Voxel deterministically converts optimized Gaussian splats into sparse active voxels, learns factorized geometry and attribute latents, and supports tiled image-conditioned generation of large aerial scenes.

### Verdict

A promising representation paper for large 3D Gaussian scenes: it obtains sparse structured latents directly from optimized splats and makes generation scale with occupied space rather than a fixed primitive budget.

### Contribution

GS-Voxel performs fitting-free conversion to active voxels while retaining sub-voxel Gaussian attributes, factorizes geometry and local appearance in a sparse VAE, and uses overlap-aware tiled flow inference for satellite-conditioned aerial generation.

### Why it matters

Most generative backbones want regular tensors, while real 3DGS assets are unordered and highly variable in size. A deterministic bridge that preserves rendering attributes could make city-scale Gaussian generation and editing substantially more tractable.

### Evidence

The abstract establishes the representation, image-conditioned flow model, and tiled large-area use case, but gives no numerical reconstruction, rendering, or generation comparison.

### Limitation

Without full-paper quantitative results, it is not yet possible to judge how much fidelity is lost during voxelization, how seams behave across tiles, or how the method compares in compute and quality with alternative structured 3DGS latents.

### Editorial note

## PACE-Bench: Benchmarking Physics Adaptation via Code Evolution in Dynamic Environments

- Authors: Yuhao Zhan, Bingxiang He, Zecong Tang, Chaojun Xiao
- Affiliations: Not provided
- Tier: Worth skimming
- Lane: General AI
- Review depth: Abstract
- Estimated reading time: 4 minutes

### Abstract

PACE-Bench tests whether self-evolving agents can repair code after physical parameters or mechanisms change across 144 source-to-target adaptation pairs in six simulation domains.

### Editorial note

PACE-Bench asks a sharper question than fixed-environment coding benchmarks: can an agent diagnose and repair a once-working design after the physics changes? Across 144 adaptation pairs, Reflexion with Qwen3-14B solves 35.9%, while GPT-5.5 reaches 66.7% only on the Statics subset. Simulator feedback helps, but memory anchors agents to early designs and broad search often fails to converge; even revealing the physical change does not eliminate the mechanism-redesign bottleneck.

## Decision-Metric Alignment in Latent World Models: Diagnostics and Action-Conditioned Objectives for MPC Planning

- Authors: Jiawei Wang, Ke Rui, Yushen Zuo, Yichun Feng, Minglei Li
- Affiliations: Not provided
- Tier: Worth skimming
- Lane: Broader vision
- Review depth: Abstract
- Estimated reading time: 4 minutes

### Abstract

This work distinguishes easy-to-probe latent information from the rank alignment needed by model-predictive control and proposes Spearman diagnostics plus action-conditioned objectives for latent world models.

### Editorial note

This paper makes a useful distinction for latent world models: a representation may decode task variables well while Euclidean latent distance still ranks candidate plans incorrectly. Plan-Real and CEM-stage Spearman diagnose that decision-metric alignment, and action-conditioned objectives reportedly improve convergence and online success despite similar probe scores. Exact task breadth, margins, and robustness to different planners require full-paper inspection.
