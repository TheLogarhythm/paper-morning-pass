# Editorial policy

## Selection

Use a quality-first judgment with no hard cap or minimum. A typical edition may contain roughly three Read first and five Worth skimming entries, but include every paper that clears the quality bar and omit weak papers even on a quiet day.

Evaluate novelty, technical substance, graphics/vision relevance, evidence quality, artifact availability, expected research usefulness, and researcher/group reputation. Reputation is supporting evidence, never a substitute for technical merit.

Among papers of similar quality, use this lane tie-break:

1. Graphics and Vision–Graphics intersections, including 3D/4D representation, rendering, geometry, reconstruction, simulation, animation, and visual computing.
2. Broader computer vision.
3. Complementary general AI, including worthwhile NLP advances.

## Evidence and writing

For Read first, provide a crisp verdict, central contribution, why it matters, concrete evidence, limitation, review depth, reading estimate, and primary-source provenance. Read the full paper or artifacts before making detailed method, result, or limitation claims; otherwise label review depth `abstract` and keep claims correspondingly cautious.

For Worth skimming, write a concise two-to-four-sentence editorial note with primary-source provenance. Do not infer affiliations, benchmark gains, code availability, venue status, or researcher reputation without evidence.

Deduplicate by arXiv ID (ignoring versions), DOI, and Hugging Face identity. A cross-list or Hugging Face appearance enriches an existing paper rather than creating a duplicate.

## Privacy

Publish only neutral topical lanes, technical tags, and evidence-backed editorial rationale. Never publish private notes, inferred research interests, personalized rejection rationales, behavioral history, or private preference weights. Personal controls remain deferred until the automation is separately accepted.
